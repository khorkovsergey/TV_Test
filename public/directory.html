Information architecture

Five doors, and everything behind them

The platform this stand is modelled on spreads roughly ninety destinations across five mega-menus and a nine-column footer. Nothing here was deleted — it was regrouped by the question a person arrived with. This page is the proof: every destination, where it now lives, and whether this prototype actually implements it.

A menu is a map

Each door opens five rows and a way to see the rest. A dropdown that lists everything is the thing being replaced, not the solution.

Depth is marked, never hidden

Advanced tools carry a PRO tag and sort lower. Hiding them behind a mode would stop a beginner ever discovering they exist.

Anything can be typed

⌘K searches every destination and every instrument. That is what makes a five-item navigation safe instead of lossy.

Company is not navigation

Pricing, careers, policies and merch keep a home in the footer, where someone looking for them expects to look.
