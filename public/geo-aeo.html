Pages that answer the question, not pages that rank

People increasingly ask an AI before they ask a platform. A page written for crawlers gets summarised badly or ignored; a page that answers one question directly, names its source and links to a real action gets cited — and brings the reader with it.

Case hypothesis NEW-04 · metric: AI citations · referral to research action
CONCEPT · ONE WORKED EXAMPLE this page carries real FAQ JSON-LD in its head — view source to see it
THE QUESTION

What does a 52-week range tell you?

Short answer. It is the highest and lowest price of the last year. Where today's price sits inside that band tells you whether the current level is unusual for this instrument — and nothing at all about where it goes next.
Why it is useful. "Expensive" and "cheap" mean nothing without a reference. A price at 95% of its yearly range is near the top of what this market has paid recently; at 5% it is near the bottom. Both are facts about the past. Neither is a signal.

source: computed from delayed daily closes of a free public feed · updated on every page load · method: (price − 52-week low) ÷ (52-week high − 52-week low)

What makes a page answerable

A direct answer in the first paragraph · the method stated · the source named · the update time shown · one unique calculation the reader cannot get elsewhere · a link into an action, not another article.

What this prototype does

Meaningful HTML before any script runs, structured headings, FAQ JSON-LD on this page, and every figure carrying its source and delay. The symbol and market pages follow the same rules.

What it does not do

Measure citations. That needs a referral pipeline and a live index, and inventing those numbers would be the exact opposite of the point this feature makes.
